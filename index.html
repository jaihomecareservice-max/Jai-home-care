Not sure where to start?

When families usually call us

If any of these sounds like your situation, home care is likely to help. Call us and we'll tell you honestly whether it is — and what it would involve.

Someone is being discharged after surgery

Dressings need changing, movement is limited, and the hospital has sent a list of instructions nobody at home feels confident following.

An elderly parent can't manage alone anymore

Bathing, toilet, meals and walking have all become difficult, and the family cannot be present through the whole day.

There has been a fall, or a fall is likely

Someone needs a steady hand for every transfer between bed, chair and bathroom before an accident sets recovery back by months.

A long-term condition needs daily tracking

BP, sugar and oxygen readings need to be taken properly and written down, so the treating doctor sees a trend instead of one-off numbers.

What we do

Every service, explained in full

Nine services across two areas of care. Each one below lists exactly what is included, so you know what you are getting before you call.

Medical & Clinical Care

Physiotherapy & Mobility Support

Guided exercise and movement therapy at home — to improve movement, reduce pain and support recovery.

  • Assisted exercise sessions
  • Joint and muscle mobility work
  • Walking and balance practice
  • Guidance for the family on safe movement
Post-Surgery Recovery Care

Structured support after an operation — dressing, exercise guidance and healing supervision at home.

  • Surgical site dressing
  • Prescribed exercise guidance
  • Healing supervision and progress checks
  • Vitals tracked through recovery
Wound Care & Dressing

Safe, hygienic dressing with follow-up monitoring for surgical wounds, bed sores and diabetic wounds.

  • Sterile cleaning and dressing changes
  • Monitoring for signs of infection
  • Scheduled follow-up visits
  • Written record of healing progress
Medical Vitals Monitoring

Routine checks recorded properly — so you and the doctor see the trend, not just one reading.

  • Blood pressure (BP)
  • Blood sugar levels
  • Pulse rate
  • Oxygen saturation (SpO₂)
  • Health records maintained for every visit
Medication Management

Medicines taken correctly and on time — especially when the list of tablets is long.

  • Dose scheduling and reminders
  • Supervision that the correct dose is taken
  • Refill tracking so medicines never run out
  • Record of what was given and when

Personal Assistance & Daily Support

Bathing & Personal Hygiene

Gentle, respectful help with daily bathing — including bed baths when needed.

  • Assisted bath or full bed bath
  • Oral, nail and hair hygiene
  • Fresh change of clothes
  • Skin care and pressure-point checks
Toileting & Grooming Support

Discreet assistance handled so the person keeps feeling like themselves.

  • Toilet and commode assistance
  • Incontinence care and changes
  • Combing, shaving and grooming
  • Help with dressing
Feeding & Meal Support

Patient help at mealtimes, at the pace the person needs.

  • Assisted feeding
  • Support with soft and liquid diets
  • Hydration reminders through the day
  • Watching for difficulty in swallowing
Mobility & Walking Assistance

Steady support for moving safely around the home.

  • Bed-to-chair and chair-to-toilet transfers
  • Supported walking indoors
  • Regular position changes to prevent bed sores
  • Spotting fall risks around the house

Ask us all of this before you decide

Any home-care provider should answer these without hesitation. Ours are answered on the first call — please do ask.

  • The total monthly amount, not just a daily rate
  • Whether any advance or deposit applies
  • What medical equipment costs to rent, per item
  • What happens if the assigned caregiver is unavailable
  • Whether rates change on Sundays or festivals
  • How much notice is needed to stop or change the care

Three commitments behind every visit we make.

01

Comfort
